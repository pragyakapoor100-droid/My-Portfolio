The Situation

The client was a well-established regional solicitors firm with three offices across the East Midlands, practicing in family law, property conveyancing, and personal injury. They had been trading for over 20 years and had a strong local reputation — but their website was generating almost no organic leads. Referrals and repeat business kept them busy, but the partners wanted to reduce dependence on word-of-mouth and build a scalable inbound channel.

The challenge was immediately apparent when I looked at the competitive landscape. The top three organic positions for every high-value query — "family solicitor Nottingham", "conveyancing solicitor Leicester", and similar — were occupied by national comparison sites (Unbiased, Checkatrade) and large multi-office law firms with Domain Authority scores in the 55–70 range. The client's DA was 18. Trying to beat them on the same terms, with the same approach, was never going to work.

"The answer wasn't to compete on their terms. It was to own the ground they couldn't be bothered to cover."

Audit Scope & Methodology

I ran a content gap and local SEO audit over five days, structured around three questions: what are people in this region actually searching for, what content exists to answer those searches, and what local signals does Google use to rank this firm that we could strengthen?

Content Gap Analysis

Using Ahrefs Keyword Explorer and GSC data, I mapped keyword demand across 340 relevant queries in family law, conveyancing, and personal injury for the three primary regions (Nottingham, Leicester, Derby). I then analysed the top 10 ranking pages for each query cluster to understand what content depth and format Google was rewarding.

Topical Authority Assessment

I audited every existing page on the site (62 pages total) for content depth, keyword alignment, and internal linking coherence. I mapped the site's existing topical coverage against the full keyword universe to identify gaps where the firm had genuine expertise but no web presence.

Local SEO & Google Business Profile Audit

Full audit of all three Google Business Profile listings: completeness score, category accuracy, review volume and recency, photo quality, Q&A section, and posting activity. I also checked NAP (name, address, phone) consistency across all major citation directories and identified inconsistencies that were diluting local ranking signals.

Key Findings

01

Targeting Saturated National Keywords, Ignoring Local Long-Tail

The existing website copy was optimised for generic national terms ("family solicitor", "conveyancing quote") where competing against national firms was almost impossible. Meanwhile, hundreds of high-intent, lower-competition local queries were completely unaddressed: "divorce solicitor Nottingham cost", "help with child custody Leicester", "how long does conveyancing take Nottingham" — all with clear commercial intent and minimal competition.

02

No Topical Depth in Any Practice Area

Each practice area had a single landing page of approximately 400 words. Family law, which covers divorce, child custody, financial settlements, cohabitation agreements, and domestic abuse legal support, had one page. Conveyancing — covering buying, selling, remortgaging, leasehold issues, and new-build purchases — had one page. Google's systems were getting no signal that this firm had genuine depth of expertise in any area.

03

Google Business Profiles Incomplete and Inconsistent

All three GBP listings were missing: business descriptions, service lists, team photos, and recent posts. Two of the three had the wrong primary category (listed as "Legal Services" instead of the more specific "Family Law Attorney" or "Conveyancer"). NAP information was inconsistent across 14 citation directories — the Nottingham office had three different phone number formats listed across the web, which is a well-documented negative signal for local pack rankings.

04

No Schema Markup

No LocalBusiness schema, no LegalService schema, no Attorney schema, and no FAQ schema — despite the site containing several Q&A style sections. This meant Google was deriving all location and service information through text parsing rather than structured data signals, reducing confidence in the firm's relevance for geo-targeted queries.

05

Reviews Undersold and Underutilised

The firm had 74 Google reviews with an average rating of 4.8 — genuinely excellent for a professional services business. But they had no review request process in place, reviews were not being responded to, and there was no strategy to grow review volume. Competitors in the local pack had 200+ reviews each.

The Strategy: Own the Depth, Win the Local Pack

Rather than competing on high-volume generic terms, the strategy was to build comprehensive topical coverage across every sub-topic within each practice area, targeting the specific long-tail queries that local people with real legal problems were searching for. This approach — sometimes called topical authority — signals to Google that a site is the definitive resource on a subject, which lifts rankings across the entire keyword cluster, not just the pages you optimise.

Content Implementation

  1. Existing practice area pages expanded from ~400 words to 2,000–3,000 words of genuinely useful, jargon-light content written with the firm's senior solicitors and reviewed for legal accuracy
  2. 28 new supporting pages created covering specific sub-topics: "What to expect from a divorce in the UK", "How is the family home divided in a divorce?", "Conveyancing timeline: what happens and when?", "What is a completion date?", etc.
  3. Each supporting page linked to its parent practice area page and to two or three related supporting pages, creating a proper topical cluster architecture
  4. Each page optimised for one primary keyword cluster with natural semantic variation — no keyword stuffing, no exact-match repetition

Local SEO & GBP Overhaul

  1. All three GBP listings updated: correct primary and secondary categories, full service lists added, 200-word business descriptions written for each office
  2. NAP data audited and corrected across all 14 citation directories via manual outreach
  3. LegalService, LocalBusiness, and FAQ schema implemented on all relevant pages
  4. Review request email sequence implemented: sent to all clients on matter completion, with a direct link to the GBP review page
  5. Weekly GBP post schedule established covering legal tips, team news, and seasonal content

What I Learned

Local professional services — law firms, accountants, dentists, financial advisers — are one of the areas where a focused content strategy can produce disproportionate results in a relatively short time. The national comparison sites that dominate generic terms rarely go deep on location-specific or situation-specific queries. That gap is where smaller, genuine practitioners can win.

The review growth was also a reminder that the most powerful local SEO interventions are sometimes the simplest. The firm's solicitors were doing exceptional work — clients were happy to leave reviews when asked. They just weren't being asked. A two-sentence email at matter completion, sent consistently, doubled their review count in four months.
