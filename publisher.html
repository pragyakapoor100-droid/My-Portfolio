The Situation

On a Tuesday morning, the founder of a mid-sized online publishing business — a lifestyle and culture site with over 200,000 monthly readers — opened Google Search Console to find a message they'd never seen before: a manual action notification. "Unnatural links to your site." By that afternoon, their organic traffic had dropped by 80%.

The founder's first call was to their SEO agency. The agency denied any knowledge of link building. Their second call was to me. Within the first two hours of looking at the backlink profile, it was clear what had happened: a systematic private blog network (PBN) link building campaign, almost certainly run by the agency, using hundreds of expired domains to point links at the client's site. The anchors were aggressively optimised — exact match commercial keywords repeated across thousands of low-quality "articles" on sites that appeared to have been dormant for years before suddenly becoming active with SEO content.

"The agency denied everything. The link profile told a different story — and it told it clearly."

What Is a Manual Penalty?

A Google manual action is different from an algorithmic penalty. It means a human member of Google's webspam team has reviewed the site and determined it is violating Google's link scheme policies. The consequences are severe: pages impacted by the manual action are suppressed in search results, often dramatically. Recovery requires not just fixing the problem, but submitting a reconsideration request to Google and waiting for a human reviewer to verify the issue is resolved.

This particular penalty was a "partial match" manual action — meaning it was applied to specific sections of the site rather than the entire domain. In practice, however, the affected sections included the site's top-performing commercial content, which accounted for the majority of its organic traffic and advertising revenue.

Audit Scope & Methodology

This engagement required two parallel workstreams: the backlink audit to identify and document the toxic links, and the reconsideration request process which requires meticulous documentation of every step taken.

Full Backlink Profile Extraction

I pulled the complete backlink profile using four tools in combination: Ahrefs, Majestic, Google Search Console's link report, and the Disavow Tool's existing file (which was empty — the agency had done nothing). The combined dataset contained over 41,000 backlinks from approximately 8,800 referring domains. I merged, deduplicated, and normalised this data into a single master spreadsheet.

Toxic Link Classification

Rather than relying solely on automated spam scores, I manually reviewed every referring domain flagged as potentially toxic. I classified links across five categories: clearly toxic (PBN, foreign spam, link farms), probably toxic (very low-quality sites with no obvious editorial purpose), borderline (low quality but not obviously manipulative), neutral (irrelevant but not harmful), and positive (genuine editorial links). This manual classification took two full days but produced a disavow file I could defend to Google's reviewers with specific reasoning for each domain included.

Pattern Identification

The PBN network became obvious through pattern analysis: 340 referring domains had been registered within a 6-week window approximately 18 months ago, all had identical WordPress themes, all had published their first and only content within days of the client's links going live, and the anchor text distribution showed extreme over-optimisation — 67% of all anchors contained exact-match commercial keywords, which is far outside what a natural link profile looks like.

Key Findings

01

1,247 Links From an Organised PBN Network

340 domains operating as a private blog network, generating 1,247 individual links to the client's site. The domains had been purchased as expired domains with residual authority, then populated with spun or AI-generated content to give the appearance of legitimate sites. All were pointing at commercial pages with keyword-rich anchors like "best budget laptops UK" and "cheap broadband deals 2023."

02

Anchor Text Distribution Catastrophically Over-Optimised

A natural backlink profile typically has fewer than 5% exact-match commercial anchors — the rest being branded, URL-based, generic ("click here", "read more"), or partial-match. This site's profile was 67% exact-match commercial. Even setting aside the PBN links, the cumulative anchor text signal was a textbook example of what Google's algorithms and human reviewers are trained to identify as manipulation.

03

Additional Toxic Links Beyond the PBN

Beyond the organised PBN, I identified a further 1,800+ links from low-quality foreign-language directories, comment spam, and link farm sites — apparently the result of negative SEO from a competitor, or the residue of very old black-hat link building from a previous SEO provider years earlier. These were not the cause of the manual action but would suppress recovery if left in place.

04

No Disavow File in Place

Despite years of link building activity and a clearly problematic profile, the previous agency had never submitted a disavow file. This meant Google had been indexing the full toxic link profile without any signal from the site owner that these links were undesired.

The Recovery Plan

Step 1 — Link Outreach (Week 1–2)

Before submitting a disavow file, Google's guidelines require a good-faith effort to have toxic links removed directly. I conducted outreach to the webmasters of every toxic domain for which a contact email could be found — approximately 180 of the 340 PBN domains. As expected, the vast majority did not respond (PBN operators do not want to be identified), but the outreach documentation was essential for the reconsideration request to show Google we had tried.

Step 2 — Disavow File Preparation (Week 2–3)

I compiled a disavow file covering all domains identified as toxic, with a companion spreadsheet documenting the classification rationale for each included domain. The final disavow file contained 487 domains — disavowing at domain level rather than URL level to catch any future links from the same networks. The file was reviewed, submitted, and confirmation of processing was tracked in GSC.

Step 3 — Reconsideration Request (Week 3)

The reconsideration request is a written statement to Google explaining: what happened, how you discovered it, what steps you took to resolve it, and what you are doing to prevent recurrence. I drafted a 1,400-word reconsideration request on behalf of the client, including:

  1. A clear, factual account of the PBN discovery and the agency relationship
  2. Documentation of all outreach attempts with dates and response statuses
  3. The disavow file and an explanation of the classification methodology
  4. A statement of commitment to editorial link building practices going forward

Step 4 — Clean Link Building (Weeks 4–9)

While waiting for Google's review (which can take 2–6 weeks), I began building clean replacement links through editorial outreach: guest contributions to relevant industry publications, digital PR pitches around the client's original research, and reclaiming unlinked brand mentions across the web. This ensured that when the penalty was lifted, the profile had positive momentum rather than just the absence of toxic links.

The manual action was lifted on day 49 after the reconsideration request was submitted — faster than the typical 6-week window, which I attribute to the quality of documentation and the clarity of the disavow file. Within two weeks of the penalty being lifted, traffic had recovered to pre-penalty levels. The new editorial links built during the waiting period gave the profile genuine momentum, which is likely why rankings ultimately settled above their pre-penalty baseline.

What I Learned

Penalty recovery is one of the most stressful SEO engagements — for the client, whose revenue is in freefall, and for the specialist, who is asking Google to trust them and waiting on a timeline they can't control. The most important thing I can do in that situation is be meticulous, be transparent with the client about timelines, and not make promises about how quickly Google will respond.

This engagement also reinforced how important it is to audit the backlink profile of any site before inheriting an SEO relationship. The damage here was built up over 18 months without anyone looking at what the agency was doing. A basic Ahrefs review every quarter would have spotted the PBN pattern within the first few months, long before a manual action was triggered.

Finally: the clean link building done during the recovery window was not wasted time. Waiting for Google without doing anything constructive is a mistake. Every legitimate editorial link built during the waiting period counted towards the eventual bounce in rankings beyond the pre-penalty baseline.

Dealing With a Penalty or Suspicious Link Profile?

Don't wait for the manual action notification. A backlink audit now is dramatically cheaper and less stressful than a penalty recovery later.
